🧪 Activity 1.1Unit I

Observation of a Real-Life Problem

⏱ 45 min👥 Individual📘 § 1.6
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Venue:

🎯 Objective

Learn to observe real users in their natural setting without judging — collecting raw, unbiased data about what people actually do.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Notebook + pen
  • ☐ Phone notes app
  • ☐ Quiet public place
  • ☐ 30 min unbroken time

📋 Steps

  1. Choose a public place — canteen, bus stop, mall, or park.
  2. Sit quietly at a distance. Do not interact with anyone.
  3. Watch for 20 minutes. Record only what you see.
  4. Note anything that surprised you or looked like a struggle.
  5. Write 5 concrete observations — facts, not opinions.

👀 What to Observe

· Where do people pause? · What workarounds do they invent? · What do they complain about? · What body language signals frustration?

  • Observing for too short a time.

🧪 Activity 1.2Unit I

Conducting User Interviews

⏱ 60 min👥 Pairs📘 § 1.9
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Interviewee:

🎯 Objective

Practise open-ended interviewing to uncover real user needs — not just opinions or wishes.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Interview guide (7 questions)
  • ☐ Notebook or recorder
  • ☐ Consent script
  • ☐ One real user

📋 Steps

  1. Write 7 open-ended questions. Avoid yes/no. Start with "Tell me about…".
  2. Ask for informed consent before recording.
  3. Interview for 20–30 minutes. Listen more than you talk.
  4. Ask "Why?" for every answer at least once.
  5. Record key quotes verbatim, not summaries.
  6. Thank the person. Offer to share findings.

🧪 Activity 1.3Unit I

Preparing an Empathy Map

⏱ 40 min👥 Individual📘 § 1.10
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
User:

🎯 Objective

Organise user research into a four-quadrant empathy map — capturing what the user says, thinks, does, and feels.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Interview notes
  • ☐ A4 sheet split into 4 quadrants
  • ☐ Sticky notes (optional)
  • ☐ Pen

📋 Steps

  1. Draw a large cross on the paper to make 4 quadrants: Says, Thinks, Does, Feels.
  2. Review your interview or observation notes.
  3. Write quotes under "Says" — exact words only.
  4. Write what the user was probably thinking under "Thinks".
  5. Write their actions under "Does".
  6. Write the emotions you noticed under "Feels".

🗺 My Empathy Map

💬SAYS
 
🧠THINKS
 
🏃DOES
 
❤️FEELS
 

🧠 Reflection

1. Where did "Says" differ most from "Thinks"?
2. Which quadrant revealed the deepest need?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Mixing "Thinks" with "Says".
  • Writing assumptions instead of research evidence.
  • Filling all quadrants with the same idea.

🧪 Activity 1.4Unit I

Developing a Persona

⏱ 45 min👥 Individual📘 § 1.11
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Persona:

🎯 Objective

Create a fictional but realistic persona that represents a user group — so design decisions can be made from the user's perspective.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Empathy map (Activity 1.3)
  • ☐ A4 sheet
  • ☐ Colours (optional)
  • ☐ Reference photos or icons

📋 Steps

  1. Review your empathy map and interview notes.
  2. Choose one main user to base the persona on.
  3. Give them a name, age, job, and location.
  4. Write their goal — what are they trying to achieve?
  5. Write their top 3 frustrations.
  6. Write one quote that captures their attitude.

🧠 Reflection

1. Which part of the persona felt most real?
2. How would this persona change a design decision?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Making a persona too generic ("a student").
  • Inventing details that contradict the research.
  • Forgetting the persona's pain points.

🧪 Activity 1.5Unit I

Preparing a User Journey Map

⏱ 60 min👥 Individual / Group📘 § 1.11
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Persona used:

🎯 Objective

Map the steps a user goes through when interacting with a product or service — and identify where the biggest pain points occur.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Persona (Activity 1.4)
  • ☐ A3 sheet or whiteboard
  • ☐ Sticky notes
  • ☐ Coloured markers

📋 Steps

  1. Write your persona's name at the top-left of the sheet.
  2. List the 5–6 main stages they go through.
  3. For each stage, note: what they DO, THINK, and FEEL (🙂 😐 😖).
  4. Mark the biggest pain point with a red flag.
  5. Mark the moment of highest satisfaction with a green star.
  6. Write one opportunity statement below the map.

🧠 Reflection

1. At which stage does the user feel the worst?
2. Which stage is the biggest opportunity for innovation?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Designing the journey from the company's view, not the user's.
  • Skipping emotions — the "Feels" row is the most valuable.
  • Too many stages — keep it to 5–6.
