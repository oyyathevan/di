🎯 Objective

Practise divergent thinking by generating 20–30 varied ideas for one problem — without judging any of them.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ HMW question
  • ☐ Notebook
  • ☐ Timer
  • ☐ Pen

📋 Steps

  1. Write your chosen HMW at the top of the page.
  2. Set a timer for 20 minutes.
  3. Write ideas fast — no filter, no judging.
  4. Push past 10 — the best ideas often come after 15.
  5. Vary the type: wild ideas, cheap ideas, absurd ideas, serious ideas.
  6. Stop at 30. Highlight your top 5 favourites.

🧪 Activity 3.2Unit III

SCAMPER Exercise

⏱ 40 min👥 Individual📘 § 3.2
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Product chosen:

🎯 Objective

Use the SCAMPER checklist to force 7 new angles on an existing product or process — generating ideas you wouldn't reach by free brainstorming alone.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ SCAMPER template
  • ☐ A photo or sample product
  • ☐ Pen
  • ☐ Notebook

📋 Steps

  1. Choose one existing product — e.g., a school bag or a water bottle.
  2. Write one idea per SCAMPER letter, exploring that lens only.
  3. S — Substitute one part with another.
  4. C — Combine with another product.
  5. A — Adapt an idea from another field.
  6. M — Modify shape, size, or material.
  7. P — Put to another use.
  8. E — Eliminate something unnecessary.
  9. R — Reverse an assumption or inside-out the design.
  10. Circle the 2 best SCAMPER outputs.

🧠 Reflection

1. Which SCAMPER lens was most productive?
2. Which SCAMPER lens was hardest? Why?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using SCAMPER on the whole product instead of one element at a time.
  • Writing generic answers ("make it better").
  • Skipping the "Reverse" step — often the richest.

🧪 Activity 3.3Unit III

Brainwriting Exercise

⏱ 30 min👥 Group (4–6)📘 § 3.2
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Group:

🎯 Objective

Generate ideas silently in a group to reduce shyness and groupthink — then build on others' ideas anonymously.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Sheets of paper (one per member)
  • ☐ Pens
  • ☐ Timer
  • ☐ HMW prompt

📋 Steps

  1. Sit in a circle of 4–6 people. Each person writes the HMW at the top of their sheet.
  2. For 3 minutes, everyone writes 3 ideas silently.
  3. On the signal, pass the sheet to your left.
  4. Read the new sheet. Add 3 more ideas — build, extend, or improve them.
  5. Repeat 4–5 rounds so each sheet holds 15+ ideas.
  6. Collect all sheets and read them aloud. Circle the top 10 ideas as a group.

🧠 Reflection

1. How did silent writing compare to talking?
2. Did anyone build on your idea? How did it feel?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Talking during the writing phase.
  • Writing the same idea repeatedly.
  • Not reading the previous ideas before adding.

🎯 Objective

Copy strategies from nature to solve a human problem — using biology as a ready-made design library.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Notebook
  • ☐ Nature images or books
  • ☐ Pen
  • ☐ Problem statement

📋 Steps

  1. Write your problem in a single sentence.
  2. Ask: "What in nature solves a similar problem?"
  3. List 3 plants or animals whose design/behaviour is relevant.
  4. Describe the natural strategy in one line each.
  5. Translate each strategy into a product or process idea.
  6. Circle the most buildable biomimicry idea.

AI-Assisted Idea Generation & Critical Evaluation

⏱ 40 min👥 Individual📘 § 3.6
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
AI tool used:

🎯 Objective

Use an AI tool to rapidly generate ideas, then critically evaluate which ones actually fit your user and context.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ ChatGPT / Gemini / Copilot
  • ☐ Your HMW question
  • ☐ Notebook
  • ☐ Problem statement

📋 Steps

  1. Write a clear prompt to the AI: problem + user + constraints + "give 20 ideas".
  2. Save the AI's top 10 outputs.
  3. For each idea, ask: Is it desirable? Feasible? Viable?
  4. Mark each idea ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ on the three lenses.
  5. Circle the 3 ideas that pass all three.
  6. Write one improvement to the best AI idea — make it your own.

🧪 Activity 3.6Unit III

Prepare an Idea-Selection Matrix

⏱ 45 min👥 Individual / Group📘 § 3.7 · 3.8
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Shortlist:

🎯 Objective

Score your shortlisted ideas against weighted criteria to select the strongest concepts — with reasoning that stands up to scrutiny.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Shortlist (5–8 ideas)
  • ☐ Decision-matrix sheet
  • ☐ Criteria definition
  • ☐ Calculator

📋 Steps

  1. Choose 4 criteria — e.g., Cost, Usability, Safety, Sustainability.
  2. Assign a weight to each (total = 100 %).
  3. List 5–8 ideas as columns.
  4. Score each idea on each criterion (1–5).
  5. Multiply score × weight, sum for each idea.
  6. Rank the ideas; the highest total wins.

📊 Idea-Selection Matrix

📋Criterion 1 (___ % weight)   ·   Idea A ___ · B ___ · C ___ · D ___
📋Criterion 2 (___ % weight)   ·   Idea A ___ · B ___ · C ___ · D ___
📋Criterion 3 (___ % weight)   ·   Idea A ___ · B ___ · C ___ · D ___
📋Criterion 4 (___ % weight)   ·   Idea A ___ · B ___ · C ___ · D ___
ΣTotal score:   A ___ · B ___ · C ___ · D ___

🧠 Reflection

1. Did the winning idea surprise you?
2. If you changed a weight, would the winner change?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Equal weights for all criteria — ignores real priorities.
  • Scoring without a rubric — subjective numbers.
  • Only using 2 criteria — misses trade-offs.

Select the Top 2–3 Concepts

⏱ 40 min👥 Group📘 § 3.8
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Team:

🎯 Objective

Converge from many ideas to 2–3 concepts worth prototyping — with clear justification for each choice.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Matrix results (3.6)
  • ☐ Concept canvas sheet
  • ☐ Pens
  • ☐ Product brief

📋 Steps

  1. Review the top-scoring ideas from Activity 3.6.
  2. Group similar ideas into concept clusters.
  3. Pick the strongest cluster as Concept 1.
  4. Pick the second-strongest cluster as Concept 2.
  5. If a third is clearly distinct, add it as Concept 3.
  6. For each concept, write: name · one-line pitch · user · key value.

  • Picking based on personal taste instead of criteria.
  • Skipping the pitch — teams lose clarity later.
