🧪 Prototyping, Testing & Validation

Seven activities to turn ideas into tangible prototypes — from low-fidelity models and storyboards to user testing, iteration, and documentation.

🎯 Unit Outcome

Students will be able to develop, prototype, and iteratively improve solutions using user feedback and appropriate physical or digital tools.

Develop a Low-Fidelity Prototype

⏱ 60 min👥 Individual / Group📘 § 4.1 · 4.2 · 4.4
Name:
Roll No.:

Concept:

🎯 Objective

Build a rough, cheap version of your chosen concept to test the core idea with users — before investing in high-fidelity work.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Paper + pencil
  • ☐ Cardboard / scissors
  • ☐ Sticky notes
  • ☐ Tape / glue

📋 Steps

  1. Pick the top concept from Unit III.
  2. Decide what to test — one clear question (e.g., "Is this easy to use?").
  3. Sketch the main screens or features on paper.
  4. Cut and arrange so a user can "click through" by hand.
  5. Keep it rough — no colours, no polish.
  6. Time-box to 60 minutes. Stop when you have something testable.

  • Trying to test everything at once.
  • Skipping the "one question" step.

Prepare a Storyboard

⏱ 45 min👥 Individual📘 § 4.2
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Persona:

🎯 Objective

Draw a comic-strip sequence showing how the user will experience your solution — revealing broken steps before building anything.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ A4 sheet divided into 6 panels
  • ☐ Pencil + eraser
  • ☐ Colours (optional)
  • ☐ Persona (from Unit I)

📋 Steps

  1. Write your persona's name at the top.
  2. Divide the page into 6 equal panels.
  3. Panel 1: the user's problem or trigger moment.
  4. Panels 2–5: the steps they take with your solution.
  5. Panel 6: the outcome — how the user feels at the end.
  6. Caption each panel with one short sentence.

3Step 2:

5Step 4:

🧠 Reflection

1. Which panel was hardest to draw? Why?
2. What gap in your idea did the storyboard reveal?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Drawing from the designer's view, not the user's.
  • Skipping the emotional ending — panel 6 matters most.
  • Too many steps — compress to 4–5 key moments.

⏱ 90 min👥 Group📘 § 4.3
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Type:

🎯 Objective

Turn your low-fidelity prototype into a working physical or digital model — enough fidelity for a real user to complete a task.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Cardboard / 3D printer / clay
  • ☐ Figma / Adobe XD (digital)
  • ☐ Basic tools
  • ☐ Laptop / phone

📋 Steps

  1. Choose physical (real object) or digital (clickable mock-up).
  2. Build one complete user flow — not all features.
  3. Add enough realism for real interaction (buttons work, screens navigate).
  4. Test it yourself first. Fix obvious bugs.
  5. Document with photos or a screen recording.
  6. Prepare a 30-second walkthrough for the user test.

🧪 Activity 4.4Unit IV

Conduct User Testing

⏱ 60 min👥 Group📘 § 4.6 · 4.7
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
# Testers:

🎯 Objective

Watch real users interact with your prototype and record where they succeed, struggle, or get confused — without helping them.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Prototype (Activity 4.3)
  • ☐ 3–5 real users
  • ☐ Consent script
  • ☐ Notepad / camera

📋 Steps

  1. Prepare 2–3 tasks for the user ("Order a coffee").
  2. Ask for consent to record or take notes.
  3. Give the task. Do not help. Say only "What are you thinking?"
  4. Record: time taken, errors, hesitation points, quotes.
  5. Ask 3 follow-up questions after the task.
  6. Thank the user. Repeat for 3–5 testers.

2Task:
3Task:

🧠 Reflection

1. Where did most testers struggle?
2. What did they do that you didn't expect?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Helping the tester when they get stuck.
  • Explaining the design instead of watching.
  • Only testing with friends — biased feedback.

🧪 Activity 4.5Unit IV

Collect Structured Feedback

⏱ 30 min👥 Individual📘 § 4.7
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Prototype:

🎯 Objective

Convert raw test notes into structured feedback that points to specific design changes — not vague opinions.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Test log (Activity 4.4)
  • ☐ Feedback form template
  • ☐ Sticky notes
  • ☐ Pen

📋 Steps

  1. Gather all test notes, quotes, and observations.
  2. Group them into themes (e.g., "Confusing navigation", "Missing feedback").
  3. Rate each theme's severity: 🔴 critical · 🟡 medium · 🟢 minor.
  4. For each theme, write a specific recommendation.
  5. Rank by (impact × frequency).
  6. Pick the top 3 to fix in the next iteration.

🎯 Top 3 Fixes for v2

🧠 Reflection

1. Which feedback surprised you the most?
2. Which feedback will you choose to ignore? Why?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Treating every comment as equally important.
  • Ignoring feedback that contradicts your assumptions.
  • Not converting feedback into actionable next steps.

🧪 Activity 4.6Unit IV

Modify the Prototype

⏱ 60 min👥 Group📘 § 4.8
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Prototype v#:

🎯 Objective

Iterate the prototype based on the top 3 fixes from Activity 4.5 — and produce v2 that's measurably better.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ v1 prototype
  • ☐ Top 3 fixes list
  • ☐ Same materials as v1
  • ☐ Camera (to document)

📋 Steps

  1. Place v1 next to the top 3 fixes list.
  2. Change one thing at a time so you know what caused the improvement.
  3. Fix the highest-severity issue first.
  4. Ask a peer to spot-check v2 before re-testing.
  5. Document each change with a before/after photo or screenshot.
  6. Prepare v2 for re-testing with the same 3 users.

1Change:   ·   Why:
2Change:   ·   Why:
3Change:   ·   Why:

🧠 Reflection

1. Which change was most impactful? How will you know?
2. What did you choose not to change? Why?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Changing everything at once — you lose cause and effect.
  • Adding new features instead of fixing what broke.
  • Forgetting to document the before/after.

🧪 Activity 4.7Unit IV

Prepare an Iteration Log

⏱ 30 min👥 Individual📘 § 4.9 · 4.11
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Project:

🎯 Objective

Document every version of your prototype — what changed, why, and what happened. Turns a messy process into clear evidence.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ All prototype photos
  • ☐ Test logs (4.4)
  • ☐ Feedback themes (4.5)
  • ☐ Change list (4.6)

📋 Steps

  1. List every prototype version (v1, v2, v3…) with a date.
  2. For each version, write the change made and the reason.
  3. Write the result — what happened after testing.
  4. Note the next step if the project were to continue.
  5. Attach or link photos/screenshots of each version.
  6. Write a 3-sentence summary of the whole journey.

📓 Iteration Log

v1Change:   ·   Reason:   ·   Result:
v2Change:   ·   Reason:   ·   Result:
v3Change:   ·   Reason:   ·   Result:
v4Change:   ·   Reason:   ·   Result:

📄 3-Sentence Summary

🧠 Reflection

1. Which iteration taught you the most?
2. Why is documentation important for a design team?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Only logging successful changes — failures teach more.
  • Writing "changed design" without specifics.
  • Starting the log too late — memory is unreliable.
