⏱ 30 min👥 Individual📘 § 5.1 · 5.2
Name:
Roll No.:

Category:

🎯 Objective

Train your innovation eye by classifying real products — building the vocabulary to describe any new idea you'll meet.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Notebook
  • ☐ Pen
  • ☐ Internet (optional)
  • ☐ Product list (10 items)

📋 Steps

  1. List 10 products you know — phones, cars, apps, gadgets, services.
  2. For each, decide: Incremental, Disruptive, or Radical?
  3. Write one sentence justifying the classification.
  4. Note whether each is Product / Process / Service / Social innovation.
  5. Circle the one that had the biggest impact on your daily life.

🧪 Activity 5.2Unit V

Identify Three Innovation Opportunities from a Campus/Community Problem

⏱ 45 min👥 Group📘 § 5.4
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Domain:

🎯 Objective

Look at your campus or community and spot three concrete innovation opportunities — from unmet needs to emerging technology fits.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Observation notes
  • ☐ Notebook
  • ☐ Camera (optional)
  • ☐ Team discussion space

📋 Steps

  1. Walk around campus or your community for 20 minutes.
  2. Note problems people face — queues, waste, delays, confusion, cost.
  3. Group similar problems into 3 clear opportunity areas.
  4. For each, write: who is affected + what's the pain + why now.
  5. Score each on Need (1–5) and Opportunity (1–5).
  6. Pick the one with the highest combined score as your lead.

🧠 Reflection

1. Which opportunity is most feasible to prototype in 4 weeks?
2. Which opportunity could scale beyond campus?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Only looking for "tech" problems — many innovations are service or process.
  • Ignoring "why now" — timing is often what makes an opportunity real.
  • Writing vague problems ("campus is messy").

Prepare a Value Proposition Canvas

⏱ 50 min👥 Individual / Group📘 § 5.5
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Solution:

🎯 Objective

Map your solution's value against real customer needs using the Value Proposition Canvas — the tool that proves product-market fit.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ VPC template
  • ☐ Interview notes
  • ☐ Persona (from Unit I)
  • ☐ Pen

📋 Steps

  1. Left side — Customer Profile: list Jobs (tasks), Pains (frustrations), Gains (desires).
  2. Right side — Value Map: list Products (what you offer), Pain Relievers (how you reduce pain), Gain Creators (how you create gain).
  3. Draw lines matching each Pain with a Pain Reliever.
  4. Draw lines matching each Gain with a Gain Creator.
  5. Circle the pairs with the strongest fit — that's your true value proposition.
  6. Write the value proposition in one sentence at the bottom.

1. Which pair (Pain–Reliever or Gain–Creator) had the strongest fit?
2. Where did the fit feel weak? What might you need to change?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Writing generic pains ("people want it cheaper").
  • Filling the map with features, not value.
  • Ignoring weak fits instead of fixing them.

🧪 Activity 5.4Unit V

Validate the Proposed Innovation with Potential Users

⏱ 60 min👥 Group📘 § 5.7
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Solution:

🎯 Objective

Test your proposed innovation with real users — check desirability (do they want it?) and viability (would they pay?) before building further.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Prototype or concept sheet
  • ☐ VPC from Activity 5.3
  • ☐ 5 target users
  • ☐ Consent script

📋 Steps

  1. Show your prototype or concept to 5 potential users.
  2. Ask three questions: (1) Would you use it? (2) How often? (3) Would you pay? How much?
  3. Record their answers verbatim.
  4. Tally "yes / no" responses for each question.
  5. Write one insight that changed your thinking.
  6. Note one design change the feedback suggests.

📊 Validation Summary

1Would use?   Yes ___ / No ___
2How often?
 
3Would pay?   Yes ___ / No ___   ·   Amount: ₹___
4One key quote:
 

🧠 Reflection

1. Did willingness-to-pay surprise you?
2. What would you change before the next test?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Asking leading questions ("Wouldn't you love this?").
  • Counting compliments, not payments or pre-orders.
  • Testing only with friends — they say yes to be nice.

Prepare an Introductory Business Model Canvas

⏱ 50 min👥 Group📘 § 5.6
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Venture:

🎯 Objective

Sketch the 9 blocks of the Business Model Canvas for your innovation — turning an idea into a business that can sustain itself.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ BMC template
  • ☐ VPC (from 5.3)
  • ☐ Validation notes (5.4)
  • ☐ Pen

📋 Steps

  1. Start with Customer Segments — who exactly?
  2. Fill Value Propositions — from your VPC.
  3. List Channels — how will customers find you?
  4. Define Customer Relationships — how do you keep them?
  5. Estimate Revenue Streams — how does money come in?
  6. List Key Resources + Key Activities + Key Partnerships.
  7. Add Cost Structure — what does it cost to run?
  8. Estimate a rough break-even — units × price vs fixed cost.

  • Filling "everyone" as the customer segment.
  • Listing multiple revenue streams that don't exist yet.
  • Skipping cost structure — dreams without bills.

🧪 Activity 5.6Unit V

Identify Possible IPR Protection

⏱ 30 min👥 Individual📘 § 5.8
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Project:

🎯 Objective

Identify which parts of your innovation could be protected by IPR — and which cannot — before sharing it publicly.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Project documents
  • ☐ IPR reference notes
  • ☐ Notebook
  • ☐ Pen

📋 Steps

  1. List every distinct element of your innovation — name, logo, design, code, process, etc.
  2. For each element, ask: is it novel? is it an invention, brand, creative work, or appearance?
  3. Match each to the correct IPR type: Patent · Trademark · Copyright · Design.
  4. Mark elements that cannot be protected (e.g., abstract ideas).
  5. Prioritise which to protect first (usually the brand name and the core invention).
  6. Write one caution — what could accidentally leak or be copied.

🧠 Reflection

1. Which IPR would you file first? Why?
2. What is the risk of not protecting at least the brand and code?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Believing an "idea" can be patented — only specific implementations can.
  • Forgetting trademarks — brand names matter long-term.
  • Publicly sharing before filing — kills novelty.

🧪 Activity 5.7Unit V

Prepare and Deliver a 3-Minute Innovation Pitch

⏱ 90 min👥 Group📘 § 5.10 · 5.11
Name:
Roll No.:
Date:
Team:

🎯 Objective

Convert weeks of design work into a clear, 3-minute pitch that wins attention — structure, delivery, and a strong ask.

🧰 Materials

  • ☐ Pitch outline (6 beats)
  • ☐ 5–8 slides or a storyboard
  • ☐ Timer
  • ☐ Rehearsal space

📋 Steps

  1. Write the six beats: Problem · Solution · Market · Model · Team · Ask.
  2. Give each beat ~30 seconds. Time yourself.
  3. Use one image per beat — no text walls.
  4. Rehearse out loud 3 times. Cut anything that isn't essential.
  5. Deliver to 5 peers. Ask for one improvement.
  6. Refine and deliver again. Final version is recorded for feedback.

❓Problem (30s):
💡Solution (30s):
📊Market (30s):
💰Model (30s):
👥Team (30s):
🙏Ask (30s):

🧠 Reflection

1. Which beat was hardest to compress? Why?
2. What feedback would you act on for a re-pitch?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Spending 2 minutes on the problem, 10 seconds on the solution.
  • Slides overloaded with text.
  • No clear ask — investors don't know what to say yes to.
