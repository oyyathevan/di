🎨 Studio Mode

Pick a real problem and walk through the entire Design Thinking process — one step at a time. Everything you type is saved automatically.

❤️ Stage 1 — Empathize

Understand your user before you design anything. Talk to them, watch them, feel their pain.

✓
Step 1.1Who is your user?

Describe the person who faces this problem. Age, role, context — make them real.

💡 Hint: Be specific. “Students” is too broad. “Second-year students with 20-minute lunch breaks” is useful.
📘 Learn: Understanding Users & Stakeholders (Unit I § 1.7) →
✓
Step 1.2What did you learn from talking to them?

Interview 3–5 real users. Write the most surprising things they said.

💡 Hint: Capture exact words. Quotes carry emotion better than summaries.
📘 Learn: Interviews & Ethics (Unit I § 1.9) →
✓
Step 1.3Empathy Map

Fill in four things about your user — one line each: what they SAY, THINK, DO, and FEEL.

💡 Hint: Thinks and Feels are usually different from Says. That's where the gold is.
📘 Learn: Empathy Mapping (Unit I § 1.10) →
✓
Step 1.4Create a Persona

Give your main user a name, age, goal and one key frustration.

📘 Learn: Personas (Unit I § 1.11) →
✓
Step 1.5User Journey

List the 5–6 steps your user takes. Note where they feel the most pain.

📘 Learn: User Journey Map (Unit I § 1.11) →

🎯 Stage 2 — Define

Turn raw research into a clear problem statement. Vague problems produce vague solutions.

✓
Step 2.1Affinity Map

Group your research notes into 3–4 themes.

📘 Learn: Affinity Mapping (Unit II § 2.1) →
✓
Step 2.2POV Statement

Fill in: [User] needs [need] because [insight].

💡 Hint: The “because” should come from real research — not a guess.
📘 Learn: POV Statements (Unit II § 2.3) →
✓
Step 2.3HMW Questions

Write 3 “How Might We…” questions that could lead to solutions.

📘 Learn: HMW Questions (Unit II § 2.3) →
✓
Step 2.45 Whys (Root Cause)

Ask “Why?” 5 times from the surface problem to the root cause.

📘 Learn: 5 Whys (Unit II § 2.4) →
✓
Step 2.5Final Problem Statement

One sentence. Clear. Specific. Solvable.

📘 Learn: Problem Validation (Unit II § 2.6) →

🎨 Stage 3 — Ideate

Generate many ideas first, judge later. Bad ideas lead to good ones.

✓
Step 3.1Brainstorm 20 Ideas

Quickly write 20 rough ideas. Don’t judge. Silly ideas are welcome.

💡 Hint: Use SCAMPER if you get stuck.
📘 Learn: Brainstorming & SCAMPER (Unit III § 3.2) →
✓
Step 3.2SCAMPER Pass

Take your best idea and push it through SCAMPER (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to use, Eliminate, Reverse).

📘 Learn: SCAMPER (Unit III § 3.2) →
✓
Step 3.3Pick Top 3 Concepts

Choose three strongest concepts. Give each a one-line pitch.

📘 Learn: Impact-Feasibility (Unit III § 3.7) →
✓
Step 3.4Decision Matrix

Score each concept on Cost, Usability, Speed (1–5). Pick the winner.

📘 Learn: Decision Matrix (Unit III § 3.8) →

🧪 Stage 4 — Prototype

Build a rough version fast. Paper is fine. Ugly is fine. Learning is the goal.

✓
Step 4.1Storyboard

Sketch (in words) 4–5 panels of how the user would use your solution.

📘 Learn: Storyboards (Unit IV § 4.2) →
✓
Step 4.2Low-Fidelity Prototype

Describe what you built — a paper sketch, a Figma mock, a cardboard model, etc.

📘 Learn: Low-Fi vs High-Fi (Unit IV § 4.4) →
✓
Step 4.3Minimum Viable Prototype

What is the smallest version you could test this week?

📘 Learn: MVP (Unit IV § 4.5) →

🎯 Stage 5 — Test

Put your prototype in front of real users. Watch. Listen. Take notes. Improve.

✓
Step 5.1Who tested it?

How many users? What happened?

📘 Learn: User Testing (Unit IV § 4.7) →
✓
Step 5.2What did they say / do?

Capture 3–5 concrete observations. Not opinions — behavior.

📘 Learn: Think-Aloud (Unit IV § 4.7) →
✓
Step 5.3What did you change?

Based on feedback, list what you changed in v2.

📘 Learn: Iteration Log (Unit IV § 4.9) →

🏆 Stage 6 — Pitch

Package your work into a clear, compelling 3-minute story. Innovation only counts when it reaches users.

✓
Step 6.1Value Proposition Canvas

List your user's Jobs, Pains, Gains — and how your product addresses them.

📘 Learn: VPC (Unit V § 5.5) →
✓
Step 6.2Business Model Canvas (lite)

Fill in the 4 most important blocks: Customers, Value, Revenue, Cost.

📘 Learn: BMC (Unit V § 5.6) →
✓
Step 6.3IPR Check

What could you protect? Patent? Trademark? Copyright?

📘 Learn: IPR (Unit V § 5.8) →
✓
Step 6.4Your 3-Minute Pitch

Write your pitch in 6 beats: Problem · Solution · Market · Model · Team · Ask.

📘 Learn: Innovation Pitch (Unit V § 5.10) →
